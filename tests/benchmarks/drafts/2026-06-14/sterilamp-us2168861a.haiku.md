## The Invention

Patent US2168861A, titled "Stroboscopic Lamp" and assigned to Westinghouse Electric & Mfg Co, was applied for on June 30, 1934, and granted on August 8, 1939. The patent falls in CPC class H01J — electric discharge tubes and discharge lamps — covering gas-filled discharge tube designs optimized for rapid pulsing. Stroboscopic lamps produce intense, brief flashes of light through controlled electrical discharge in a gas-filled envelope, with precise management of gas pressure, electrode spacing, and discharge timing. The patent specifies discharge characteristics enabling both high flash frequency and synchronized timing applications.

## The Mark

STERILAMP was filed by Westinghouse Electric Corporation on August 25, 1939 — seventeen days after the stroboscopic lamp patent grant. The goods description identifies "vacuum or gas-filled tubes employed for the generation of radiations for hastening the tenderization of meat, in showcases and domestic refrigerators." This description indicates a germicidal ultraviolet lamp product marketed to food retailers and cold-storage operators. Both stroboscopic and ultraviolet germicidal lamps operate on the same H01J discharge tube principle: electrical discharge through a controlled gas or vacuum produces radiant energy with specific spectral and temporal characteristics. The mark was registered as No. 0375857.

## The Connection

US2168861A and the STERILAMP trademark represent parallel commercial applications of the same discharge tube technology class. Westinghouse's discharge lamp research program in the mid-1930s produced both pulsed stroboscopic designs and continuous ultraviolet output designs, drawing on shared engineering knowledge of gas discharge physics. The filing sequence—patent grant on August 8, trademark application on August 25—reflects Westinghouse's coordination between patent prosecution closure and commercial trademark registration, indicating product launch timing aligned with patent protection maturity. The five-year interval between patent application (1934) and simultaneous grant and trademark filing (1939) indicates sustained development before commercialization.

## Historical Context

By 1939 Westinghouse held a commanding position in specialty lamp manufacture alongside General Electric, with particular strength in gas discharge tube products. Public health advocacy and expanded commercial cold-storage infrastructure created growing demand for ultraviolet germicidal equipment in the late 1930s. STERILAMP targeted a specific industrial niche: meat display cases and refrigerated storage, where UV treatment extended shelf life and reduced bacterial load without chemical additives. The August 1939 patent-trademark filing marks Westinghouse's formalized commercial entry into the germicidal lamp market.

This pair demonstrates Westinghouse's deliberate coordination of patent and trademark filing to protect both the underlying technical invention and commercial product identity.
