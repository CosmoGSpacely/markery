## The Invention

Patent US2168861A, titled "Stroboscopic Lamp" and assigned to Westinghouse Electric & Mfg Co, was applied for on June 30, 1934, and granted on August 8, 1939. The patent covers gas-filled discharge tube designs falling within CPC class H01J — electric discharge tubes and discharge lamps. A stroboscopic lamp generates brief, high-intensity flashes of light by discharging electrical energy through a gas-filled chamber in rapid, controlled pulses; the technical specification and performance depend on precise management of gas pressure, electrode spacing, and discharge timing. The patent represents five years of refinement from application to grant, typical of complex electrical device prosecution in the late 1930s.

## The Mark

STERILAMP (Trademark Serial No. 71423019) was filed by Westinghouse Electric & Manufacturing Company on August 25, 1939 — seventeen days after the stroboscopic lamp patent was granted. The goods statement specifies "vacuum or gas-filled tubes employed for the generation of radiations for hastening the tenderization of meat, in showcases and domestic refrigerators." The product was Westinghouse's ultraviolet germicidal discharge lamp, marketed for use in food retail display cases and cold storage installations where UV radiation inhibited bacterial growth without chemical additives. The mark was registered as No. 0375857.

## The Connection

Both the stroboscopic lamp patent and the STERILAMP product are gas-filled discharge tubes in CPC class H01J; they embody the same fundamental electrical discharge technology applied to different commercial purposes. The seventeen-day interval between patent grant and trademark filing indicates that Westinghouse's patent and trademark counsel coordinated their filings to protect the technical invention and secure the commercial brand name simultaneously as the product entered market. The stroboscopic patent, pending since 1934, had matured through Westinghouse's engineering development cycle in parallel with the company's market planning for a branded UV germicidal lamp product.

## Historical Context

By 1939, Westinghouse was competing with General Electric across the spectrum of specialty lamp technology. The late 1930s witnessed growing commercial demand for ultraviolet germicidal lamps as food safety concerns and the expansion of cold storage infrastructure created industrial markets for radiation-based preservation. The STERILAMP trademark explicitly targets meat display cases and domestic refrigerators — retail and commercial food-handling environments where continuous UV germicidal output provided a competitive advantage over chemical preservation methods. The August 1939 filing date marks Westinghouse's commercial entry into the germicidal lamp category.

## Significance

This pair demonstrates Westinghouse's systematic approach to protecting new discharge tube technology across both patent and trademark channels. The minimal filing gap reflects deliberate coordination between the company's legal departments, a practice that indicates high confidence in the product's imminent commercial viability. The STERILAMP case also reveals the breadth of Westinghouse's vacuum tube portfolio: even as the company dominated radio receiver components, it was simultaneously developing industrial and consumer applications — germicidal sterilization, stroboscopic timing — that leveraged the same core discharge tube engineering. The trademark goods statement naming specific end uses (showcases, refrigerators) documents Westinghouse's early market positioning in the ultraviolet germicidal lamp sector.
