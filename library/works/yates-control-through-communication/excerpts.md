# Excerpts — Control Through Communication

*Yates, JoAnne, 1989. Johns Hopkins University Press.*

## Overview

Yates traces how American businesses between the 1850s and 1920s developed
systematic communication practices — filing systems, form letters, internal
reports, managerial memos — as tools of organizational control. The book's core
argument is that the rise of the modern corporation was inseparable from the rise
of systematic record-keeping: managers could not coordinate large organizations
without information flows, and information flows required physical filing and
indexing infrastructure.

Directly relevant to the information-systems project: Yates documents the card
filing and visible index systems that Remington Rand's predecessors developed and
sold, situating them within the broader managerial revolution. The KARDEX visible
card system appears as a representative technology of systematic office practice.
The book provides the secondary-source grounding that makes KARDEX a viable
Wikipedia article subject.
