# Excerpts — The Book of Radio

*Taussig, Charles William, 1922. Boni & Liveright.*

## Passages

### KDKA — Westinghouse's first broadcast station

> "This is KDKA talking; KDKA, the broadcasting station of the Westinghouse Electric and Manufacturing Company, East Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania." (pp. 2–3)

Context: Contemporary (1922) documentation of KDKA as Westinghouse's broadcast station — the first licensed commercial broadcast station in the US (1920). Directly relevant to the Westinghouse entity and the commercial context for Westinghouse's radio-era patents and trademarks.

### Vacuum tube as the most important radio device

> "Most important device in modern radio — Not foolproof — Two distinctive types of vacuum tube" (p. 95)

Context: 1922 characterisation of vacuum tubes as the central radio technology — establishes the commercial significance of the H01J class patents in radio-pioneers and contemporaneous with the filing of the VICTOR radio apparatus trademark by RCA.

### The Westinghouse receiver

> "The Westinghouse receiver — Hissing noise — Eliminating the whistling noise" (p. 148)

Context: Contemporary reference to a specific Westinghouse consumer receiver product — direct evidence of Westinghouse's radio set manufacturing in 1922, relevant to the Westinghouse entity's trademark filings for radio apparatus.

### RCA transatlantic receiving station

> "Two receiving sets of the Radio Corporation Trans-atlantic Service, Riverhead, Long Island . . ." (p. 324)

Context: Contemporary documentation of RCA's receiving infrastructure — establishes RCA's operational scale in 1922, the year before the radio apparatus trademark and vacuum tube patents in radio-pioneers were filed.
