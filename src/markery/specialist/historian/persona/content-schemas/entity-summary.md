# Content Schema: Entity Summary

## Purpose

A single-page summary of one entity: who they were, what they made, how they appear in the patent and trademark record, and what the confirmed pairs reveal about their commercial strategy.

## Data Provided by Backend

The site builder supplies:
- Entity record (canonical name, type, industry, name variants, notes)
- Aggregate statistics: patent count, trademark count, confirmed match count, active date range
- Name variants table: all known spellings by source (patent assignee vs. trademark owner)
- Link grid: trademark gallery filtered to this entity, patent gallery filtered to this entity, confirmed matches

## What the Historian Writes

**Output file:** `projects/<project>/content/entity-<slug>.md`

Where `<slug>` is the canonical name lowercased with spaces replaced by hyphens (e.g., `entity-remington-rand.md`, `entity-wilson-jones.md`).

### Required sections (in order)

#### 1. Historical identity (100–200 words)

Who was this company? Establish:
- What it made and sold — specifically, not generically
- When it operated (founding, mergers, dissolution if applicable)
- Its position within the industry: leader, specialist, regional player?
- Any name changes or corporate events that appear in the filing record

Draw on the filing record as evidence, not just background knowledge. A company's self-description in its trademark goods clauses is primary evidence for what it understood itself to be.

#### 2. In the filing record (100–150 words)

What does this entity look like from the inside of the patent and trademark record?
- Under what name variants does it appear? Why (acquisitions, regional subsidiaries, name changes)?
- Are the patent assignee name and trademark owner name consistent? Discrepancies are analytically significant.
- What period does the record cover? If there are gaps, note them.

#### 3. Research significance (75–125 words)

Why is this entity in the project? What confirmed or suspected patent-trademark correspondences make it worth studying? This is a forward reference to the match pages — identify the pairs already confirmed and any promising candidates still under review.

#### 4. Links section (generated by site builder)

The site builder automatically appends:
- Trademark gallery link (filtered to this entity)
- Patent gallery link (filtered to this entity)  
- Links to each confirmed match page

Do not write this section; leave it for the site builder.

## Output Format

Plain Markdown. No YAML front matter. `##` headings. Rendered as the main body of `projects/<project>/site/entities/<slug>.html`, with the site builder wrapping it in a page header that includes the entity stats and name variants table.

## Example opening

> Remington Rand Inc. is the product of a 1927 merger between Remington Typewriter Company — one of the dominant players in American typewriter manufacturing since the 1870s — and Rand Kardex Bureau, the leading maker of visible-record card-index systems. The merged company did not simply add the two product lines together; it combined two distinct traditions of office efficiency into a single corporate identity built around the promise of systematic information management.

> The merger shows clearly in the trademark record. Before 1927, marks appear under "Remington Typewriter Company" and "Rand Kardex Bureau, Inc." as separate filers. After 1927, all new filings appear under "Remington Rand Inc." The owner field in the filing record traces this transition mark by mark.
