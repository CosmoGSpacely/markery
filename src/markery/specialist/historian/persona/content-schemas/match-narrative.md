# Content Schema: Match Narrative

## Purpose

A research essay documenting a single confirmed patent-trademark pair: the evidence that links them, what the correspondence reveals about the product's commercial life, and what it contributes to the broader project argument.

This is the historian's primary analytical output. Existing essays (`soundex.md`, `kardex.md`) already follow this schema and can be treated as exemplars.

## Data Provided by Backend

The site builder supplies:
- The confirmed match record (patent number, trademark serial, entity, note)
- Mark image (enhanced if available, raw TSDR if not)
- Patent figure (from `patent_figures` if available)
- Filing date and grant date for both records
- The date gap between patent grant and trademark filing
- Links to entity page, trademark gallery (mark highlighted), patent gallery (patent highlighted)

## What the Historian Writes

**Output file:** `projects/<project>/content/<slug>.md`

Where `<slug>` is the mark name lowercased (e.g., `soundex.md`, `kardex.md`, `vi-dex.md`). For marks with spaces or special characters, use hyphens.

### Required sections (in order)

#### 1. Opening claim (1–3 sentences)

State the correspondence directly. What is the patent, what is the trademark, and what links them? This is the thesis sentence — every subsequent paragraph provides evidence for it.

> The SOUNDEX mark (serial 71246709, filed 1927) is the commercial face of Robert Russell's 1918 phonetic coding patent (US1261167A). The patent describes a method for indexing names by sound; the trademark registers the name under which Rand Kardex Bureau sold that method as a product.

#### 2. The patent (100–200 words)

What does the patent actually describe? Go beyond the title:
- What problem was the invention solving?
- What was technically novel about the approach?
- Who were the inventors? What can be established about them?
- When was it filed and granted — what was the patent's commercial window?

Ground in the patent record. Quote the title and CPC classes. If a figure is available, describe what it shows.

#### 3. The trademark (100–200 words)

What does the filing record say about this mark?
- Serial number, filing date, first-use date, registration number if granted
- Goods description — what exactly was being sold under this mark?
- Owner at filing — is this the same entity that held the patent, or a successor?
- Mark type (word, design, stylized) — what does the choice reveal?
- Status: was it registered? Cancelled? Abandoned?

Quote the goods description directly. It is primary evidence.

#### 4. The correspondence (150–250 words)

Why do these two records describe the same product? This is the analytical core. Provide:
- Date evidence: the relationship between grant date and first-use date
- Entity evidence: are the assignee and owner the same company (or corporate successor)?
- Goods evidence: does the patent's claims scope match the trademark's goods description?
- Any additional corroborating evidence (trade press, product catalogs, other marks in the same window)

Distinguish what the record shows from what it implies. "The patent was granted nine years before the mark was filed" is evidence. "The company held the patent for nine years before commercializing it under this brand" is inference — label it as such.

#### 5. Historical significance (75–150 words)

What does this confirmed pair contribute to the research argument? How does it fit within:
- The entity's broader portfolio strategy
- The product category's development in this period
- The project's argument about the patent-trademark correspondence as a research method

This section should reach beyond the individual pair to the larger historical claim the pair supports.

#### 6. Sources (generated by site builder)

The site builder appends a Sources section with formatted citations for the USPTO records. Do not write this; the site builder generates it from the match data.

## Output Format

Plain Markdown. No YAML front matter. Prose paragraphs, not bullet lists — this is a research essay, not a structured report. `##` section headings. Tables only when directly comparing multiple records. SQL queries in code blocks when cited.
