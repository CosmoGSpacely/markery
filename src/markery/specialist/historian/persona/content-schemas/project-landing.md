# Content Schema: Project Landing Page

## Purpose

The entry point for a project's site. Orients the reader: what period, what entities, what method, what confirmed findings so far.

## Data Provided by Backend

The site builder supplies:
- Project name and entity list (from `entities.txt` and entity records)
- Confirmed match count and essay count
- Candidate count and review status
- Navigation links to all generated pages
- A summary card for each confirmed match (mark name, patent number, entity, link to essay)

## What the Historian Writes

**Output file:** `projects/<project>/content/index-narrative.md`

### Required sections (in order)

#### 1. Research scope (100–175 words)

What is this project studying? Establish:
- The historical subject: what industry, what period, what question
- The method: USPTO trademark-patent cross-reference, what that reveals, why it works for this subject
- The scope limitations: these records cover 1900–1939; the CPC classes covered and why they were chosen

Do not describe the project in terms of the software. Describe the historical research question. The reader is a historian or researcher, not a developer.

#### 2. Entities under study (one short paragraph per entity, 40–75 words)

For each entity, a brief identifying statement:
- Who they were, what they made
- Why they are in this project
- What the confirmed pairs so far reveal about them

The site builder generates entity name headers and links automatically; the historian writes only the descriptive paragraph for each.

#### 3. Method note (50–100 words)

A brief, plain-language explanation of the correspondence method for readers unfamiliar with it. What does it mean to confirm a patent-trademark pair? What evidence is required? What does confirmation imply about the historical record?

This section is skeptic-facing — assume the reader is asking "why should I trust these correspondences?"

#### 4. Confirmed findings summary (generated by site builder)

The site builder generates a card grid of confirmed matches with links to essays. The historian does not write this section.

## Output Format

Plain Markdown. No YAML front matter. `##` headings. Prose only — no bullet lists in sections 1–3. The site builder renders this as the body text of the landing page, interspersed with the data-driven components (entity stat chips, confirmed match cards, navigation).

## Example opening

> This project documents the commercial history of pre-computer information systems in the United States between 1900 and 1939, using the USPTO patent and trademark record as primary evidence. The central method is cross-reference: for each company that both patented filing-system technology and trademarked product names in the same period, we identify which patents correspond to which trademarks and what that correspondence reveals about how products moved from the inventor's bench to the commercial market.

> The research covers four companies — Remington Rand, Wilson Jones, Yawman & Erbe, and Boorum & Pease — selected because their combined patent and trademark records in CPC classes B42F and B42D are extensive enough to support systematic cross-reference while remaining tractable for close historical reading.
