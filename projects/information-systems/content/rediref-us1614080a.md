## The Invention

US1614080A, titled "Loose-Leaf Binder" and granted January 11, 1927, was assigned to the Wilson Jones Co and invented by George Washington Newman — the same engineer whose earlier work produced US1527374A (1925), the foundation of the VI-DEX product line. Newman was Wilson Jones's primary binder engineer through the mid-1920s. The patent covers an improved loose-leaf binder mechanism, addressing the engineering of the binding post and ring system that holds sheets in place while permitting their addition and removal. Loose-leaf binder technology was mature by 1927, but each refinement — a better locking mechanism, a smoother post action, a more durable ring — justified its own patent and, potentially, its own product name.

## The Mark

REDIREF (registration 0237470) covered "loose-leaf binders and loose-leaf sheets therefor." The name follows the filing trade's compounding formula: REDI (ready) + REF (reference), communicating immediate access to reference material. REDIREF's first-use date is January 5, 1927 — six days before the patent was granted. This near-simultaneity of first commercial use and patent grant is consistent with a company bringing a product to market as its patent application moved through final review: the REDIREF binder was being sold, or at least demonstrated, in the first week of January 1927, the same week the patent issued.

## The Connection

The tightest evidence links REDIREF to US1614080A through timing and entity. The patent was granted January 11, 1927; REDIREF first use was January 5, 1927; both belong to Wilson Jones. The compressed timeline suggests active coordination between engineering and commercial operations rather than the extended lag between invention and market introduction that characterized some earlier periods. The patent is the binder mechanism; the mark is the name under which Wilson Jones sold it.

## Historical Context

REDIREF was filed on 1927-09 as part of a coordinated cluster: Wilson Jones registered a companion binder mark the same day under consecutive registration numbers, and formalized VI-DEX in the same window. Rather than filing one mark per product as each launched, the company registered its loose-leaf binder product names in bulk — a single strategic decision to secure the brand portfolio at once. The consecutive registration numbers confirm the marks were processed as a unit.

## Significance

The REDIREF pair illustrates a filing strategy that recurs across this project's entity registry: the coordinated product-family launch. The September 1927 cluster of Wilson Jones trademark filings represents portfolio management, not a sequence of independent product introductions — a company securing a family of brand names around its binder engineering in a single, deliberate registration push. The patent record dates the engineering; the trademark record dates the branding; together they document how a mature product category was still generating both patents and marks well into the late 1920s.
