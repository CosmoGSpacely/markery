## The Trademark Record

The trademark gallery contains all marks filed by the four project entities between 1900 and 1939 and captured in the USPTO Trademark Case Files Dataset. The marks span twenty-five years of American office-products branding, from Boorum & Pease's "B & P" house mark (1914) through Remington Rand's KARDEX filing (1939). Seven of the eight confirmed patent-trademark pairs appear here as linked cards.

The filing timeline reveals three distinct clusters. Remington Rand's post-merger marks appear through the 1930s, with LINEDEX (1931), SCOTTIE (1934), ARISTOCRAT (1937), and VARIADEX and KARDEX (both 1939) documenting the company's successive visible-index product launches after the 1927 consolidation of Remington Typewriter and Rand Kardex Bureau. The Wilson Jones cluster concentrates in the summer and fall of 1927: VI-DEX in July, REDIREF and HANDIREF on the same day in September — three product-name marks filed in ten weeks. The Yawman & Erbe cluster appears in January and February 1930: SHANNON and two "Y AND E." marks within six weeks.

## Reading the Cards

Each card shows the mark name, filing date, registration status, goods description, and entity. Marks with confirmed patent pairs carry a link to the full match essay. The draw code in the card footer indicates the mark's visual character: 1000 is a typed wordmark; 3000 is a design mark with a distinctive graphic element; 5S11 is a stylized letterform. The SOUNDEX QUICK AS A FLASH mark (draw code 3000) is the only design mark with a lightning-bolt figure; VI-DEX (5S11) was filed as a stylized letterform. Both reflect the period's commercial enthusiasm for marks that communicated speed, precision, and modernity.

Status codes reflect the mark's final disposition in the USPTO record. Most marks in the 1900–1939 dataset have expired (status 900); a small number were abandoned or cancelled. No marks in this dataset are currently active under their original registration.
