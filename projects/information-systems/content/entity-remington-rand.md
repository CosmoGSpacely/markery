# Remington Rand

## Historical Identity

Remington Rand Inc. was formed in 1927 by the merger of Remington Typewriter Company — one of the largest typewriter manufacturers in the United States since the 1870s — and Rand Kardex Bureau, Inc., the leading maker of visible-record card-index systems. The merged company combined two distinct traditions of office efficiency: the Remington name carried decades of associations with typewriting and business-machine reliability; the Rand Kardex name brought the Kardex visible-record system, an overlapping-tray card-index that had become standard equipment in American business offices.

The merger shows clearly in the trademark record. Before 1927, marks appear under the separate filers "Remington Typewriter Company" and "Rand Kardex Bureau, Inc." After 1927, all new filings appear under "Remington Rand Inc." The first major post-merger trademark in the filing-systems category is REMRANDCO (serial 71285826, filed June 1929), a name that fuses the two corporate identities. Through the 1930s, Remington Rand registered a product family for its visible-index line: LINEDEX (1931), SCOTTIE (1934), ARISTOCRAT (1937), VARIADEX and KARDEX (both 1939). Remington Rand was later acquired by the Sperry Corporation in 1955, forming Sperry Rand.

## In the Filing Record

Remington Rand and its predecessors appear in the patent record under four assignee names — Remington Typewriter Company, Remington Typewriter Co, Remington Rand Inc, and Firm Remington Rand Inc — reflecting the company's internal reorganizations and the inconsistent name transcription typical of early patent documentation. On the trademark side, the filing record traces the full corporate succession: Remington Typewriter Company, the, files the earliest marks (KARDEX, 1920); Rand Kardex Bureau, Inc. files the SOUNDEX marks in 1927; Remington Rand Inc. carries the portfolio forward through 1939, with the subsidiary Remington Rand Business Service, Inc. registering LINEDEX in 1931.

The 171 patents assigned to this entity in filing-appliance and blank-book classes between 1917 and 1939 make it the heaviest patent filer in the information-systems project by a significant margin.

## Research Significance

Remington Rand is the central entity in the information-systems project because its combined patent and trademark record documents the full arc of visible-card-index technology from its 1918 origins (the Russell Soundex patent) through its commercial peak (the 1930s KARDEX product family). Confirmed pairs include the Russell and Odell Soundex patents linked to the SOUNDEX and SOUNDEX QUICK AS A FLASH marks (1927), and US2152606A and US2178457A linked to VARIADEX and KARDEX respectively (1939). Together they demonstrate how Remington Rand used its patent portfolio to anchor successive product-name trademarks over more than two decades.
