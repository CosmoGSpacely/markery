## The Invention

US1738120A, titled "File Folder" and granted December 3, 1929, was assigned to the Yawman & Erbe Mfg Co. The patent covers improvements to the file folder — the basic unit of the vertical filing system. Yawman & Erbe held an extensive filing-appliance patent portfolio stretching back to 1902; this patent sits within their mature filing-systems line rather than representing a technical breakthrough. The applicant date precedes the grant by several years, indicating the innovation was in development well before the trademark was filed.

## The Mark

SHANNON (registration 0272881) covered a broad goods description: "arch-type binders and file boards, arch files, hand-operated perforators, file compressors and covers for arch-type binders, indexes for arch binders, file guides, transfer cases, and loose-leaf binders." The SHANNON name did not originate with Yawman & Erbe. Shannon Brothers Ltd. of London had been manufacturing the Shannon lever-arch file since the 1880s — a distinctive arch-mechanism binder in which a metal arch rod holds perforated papers, secured by a lever-operated clasp. By 1930, when Yawman & Erbe filed the American trademark, SHANNON was already a recognized name for arch-mechanism filing in the trade. The filing registered that name in the United States under Yawman & Erbe's ownership.

## The Connection

The 51-day gap between US1738120A (December 3, 1929) and the SHANNON filing (1930-01) is consistent with a coordinated filing strategy, though the correspondence here is by product category rather than direct invention. Yawman & Erbe was a filing-equipment company; the patent covers a filing-equipment improvement; the trademark covers filing equipment. The temporal proximity indicates the company was actively securing its intellectual-property portfolio in late 1929 and early 1930, bringing both a new patent and a major brand name into formal registration within the same two-month window.

## Historical Context

The SHANNON pair requires a distinction the other pairs in this project do not: the trademark registers a brand name whose underlying technical concept was not invented by the trademark owner. The patent describes folders for vertical files; the mark covers arch-type binders — a different binding mechanism. Yawman & Erbe were adapting an established product category to their manufacturing capabilities and formalizing American rights to a name that had originated in a London workshop, not commercializing their own invention. The pairing therefore documents acquisition and adaptation rather than a direct claim-to-product correspondence.

## Significance

SHANNON is still manufactured today; lever-arch files sold under the name remain available in stationery markets worldwide, making this the only confirmed pair in the information-systems project whose commercial lineage is unbroken from the early twentieth century to the present. The Yawman & Erbe registration of January 1930 is one link in that chain — the moment an American manufacturer formalized its claim to a name that had originated abroad forty years earlier and would outlast the company that registered it.
