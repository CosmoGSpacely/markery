## The Invention

US1435663A, titled "Index" and granted November 14, 1922, was filed by Margaret K. Odell on December 19, 1921, and assigned to the Remington Typewriter Company. Odell's patent extended the foundational Soundex coding method for surname indexing: the system reduces a surname to a one-letter, three-digit code based on consonant sounds, eliminating vowels and collapsing phonetically equivalent pairs. The practical result is a filing system that handles spelling variation systematically — particularly useful for records involving immigrant names transcribed by ear. The patent's title, simply "Index," understates what it describes; it is the technical foundation on which the SOUNDEX product line, and this advertising mark, rest.

## The Mark

SOUNDEX QUICK AS A FLASH (registration 0247084) covered goods identical to the institutional SOUNDEX wordmark: blank and partially-printed index cards and forms, guide and separator cards for phonetic indexes, and holders. But the mark itself is visually unlike the sober wordmark — a design mark featuring bold script lettering bisected by a large lightning bolt, with the slogan "quick as a Flash" in cursive below. The visual vocabulary is unmistakably of its moment: speed, electricity, modernity, the same idiom appearing in radio and automobile advertising of the late 1920s. A filing system sold on efficiency needed to make efficiency feel exciting. The recorded first-use date is May 31, 1927 — more than four months before the October filing.

## The Connection

This advertising mark rests on the same Odell patent as the institutional wordmark: the goods are "of the type used for phonetic indexes," the method is US1435663A's. By 1927 both the Russell and Odell patents had passed toward the Rand Kardex Bureau as part of the corporate consolidation that would become Remington Rand. Filing this design mark in 1927-10 — after the merger was announced and before it was finalized — secured the commercial identity of the phonetic-index product line from a second angle: a technical name for purchasing agents, a bold design mark for broader advertising.

## Historical Context

The first-use date of May 31, 1927 places the slogan in active commercial use nearly five months before its formal registration, roughly concurrent with the final stages of the Remington Rand merger. The decision to register a separate advertising mark — rather than relying on the single product wordmark — reflects a company treating its phonetic-index system as a significant commercial asset worth protecting on multiple registers at once.

## Significance

Filing a family of marks around a single product — one establishing the technical product name, the other registering the advertising identity — would become standard practice in twentieth-century brand management, but it was still relatively unusual in the late 1920s for a specialist business-equipment manufacturer. The SOUNDEX QUICK AS A FLASH registration documents that deliberate dual-brand strategy in the filing record, and pins it to the Odell indexing patent that made the product work.
