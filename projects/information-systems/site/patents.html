The Patent Record

The patent gallery contains filings by the four project entities in filing-appliance and blank-book patent classes across the research period. Remington Rand leads with 171 patents — the heaviest patent filer in the project by a significant margin — reflecting both the company's scale and its sustained investment in visible-record and card-index technology through the full research period. Wilson Jones follows with 130 patents concentrated in the mid-1920s, corresponding to the peak of George W. Newman's binder-mechanism work. Yawman & Erbe contributed 59 patents in vertical-file and card-index components from 1902 forward. Boorum & Pease holds 38 patents focused on blank-book construction.

The grant timeline shows two periods of heightened activity across the four entities. The first runs from approximately 1918 to 1930, when filing-appliance technology was advancing rapidly: the Soundex patents (1918, 1922), the Wilson Jones binder patents (1925, 1927), and the Yawman & Erbe file-folder patents (1929) all cluster in this window. The second concentrates in the late 1930s with Remington Rand's visible-index mechanism patents (1939), which correspond directly to the VARIADEX and KARDEX trademark filings of the same year.

Confirmed Pairs

Eight patents in this gallery appear in confirmed pairs with trademarks in the trademark record. These are identified with a link to the full match essay. The remaining patents represent the broader portfolio each company maintained around its confirmed product lines — the mechanism improvements, variant designs, and component refinements that surrounded any significant commercial product, most of which were never linked to a specific trademark.

The ratio of paired to unpaired patents — eight paired out of roughly four hundred total — reflects both the research method and the commercial reality. Most patents cover incremental improvements rather than product-defining innovations. The confirmed pairs identify the specific technical moments that a company chose to commemorate with a product name.
