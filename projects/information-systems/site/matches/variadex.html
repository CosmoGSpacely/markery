VARIADEX: The Ten-Day Patent

The VARIADEX trademark (serial 71417978, filed April 7, 1939) was registered by Remington Rand Inc. ten days after the company was granted US2152606A, "Card Index," on March 28, 1939. No other confirmed pair in this project has a shorter gap between patent and trademark. The sequence documents, with unusual precision, the moment a new filing-system product moved from patent grant to commercial registration.

---

The Patent

US2152606A, titled "Card Index" and granted March 28, 1939, was assigned to Remington Rand Inc. of New York. The inventor was Benjamin G. Rand. The patent covers a card-index system incorporating guides — dividers that organize a set of index cards into retrievable sections, allowing rapid access to specific portions of a large file. The application predates the grant by several years, meaning the commercial product described by the VARIADEX mark was in development well before either the patent issued or the trademark was filed.

The title, "Card Index," is generic; the commercial name, VARIADEX, is specific. "Varia" from the Latin for varied or changeable; "dex" the conventional suffix for index systems in this period (KARDEX, SOUNDEX, LINEDEX). The name suggests a variable or multi-arrangement card index — one whose guide structure could be reconfigured to suit different filing schemes.

---

The Trademark

Remington Rand filed for VARIADEX on April 7, 1939, with registration number 0371824 awarded subsequently. The goods description is precise: *"card and correspondence file guides."* File guides are the divider cards within an index system — exactly the component described in US2152606A. The trademark was filed under the name REMINGTON RAND INC. of New York, consistent with all other Remington Rand filing-system marks of the late 1930s. The mark was eventually cancelled (status 900), suggesting it was absorbed into the consolidated KARDEX brand family after Remington Rand reorganized its product lines.

The draw code 0371824 indicates a word mark registered without a design element — the name alone carried the trademark, which was consistent with Remington Rand's approach to its indexed-system product line (KARDEX, LINEDEX, VARIADEX are all pure word marks).

---

The Correspondence

The correspondence between US2152606A and the VARIADEX trademark is the most tightly documented in the information-systems project. The patent was granted March 28, 1939. The trademark was filed April 7, 1939 — ten days later. This is not a case of a company registering a name years after commercial introduction; it is a near-simultaneous dual registration of the technical invention and its commercial identity.

The goods description seals the correspondence. US2152606A covers a card-index system with guides; the VARIADEX trademark covers "card and correspondence file guides." The language is not merely analogous — it is nearly the same vocabulary. Remington Rand's patent counsel and trademark counsel were clearly working from the same product specification.

The inventor, Benjamin G. Rand, shared his surname with the company's own corporate lineage — the Rand Kardex Bureau that merged with Remington Typewriter in 1927 to form Remington Rand. Whether this is coincidence or kinship is not established by the filing record alone.

---

Historical Significance

The VARIADEX pair illustrates what systematic patent-trademark practice looked like at a mature American manufacturing company in 1939. Remington Rand had been filing patents and trademarks in the filing-system classes since the late 1910s. By 1939, the process was evidently routine: patent the mechanism, register the name, within days of each other. The VARIADEX filing sits at the end of a decade of intensive visible-record innovation — 171 patents across the consolidated Remington Rand entity — and represents one of the last product-name marks the company filed before the Second World War restructured its business priorities.
