## The Invention

The patent discloses a detachable camera back designed to hold both photographic film and glass plates securely while allowing rapid removal for processing. It incorporates a spring‑biased latch mechanism that aligns the film plane precisely with the camera’s focal plane, reducing exposure errors. The construction includes a light‑tight sealing system to prevent fogging during loading and unloading. Together, these features aim to improve workflow efficiency for professional photographers using large‑format equipment.

## The Mark

The “RITEWAY” trademark was registered in Class 5 for photographic film and plate holders, indicating that Graflex marketed the detachable backs under this brand name. The mark appears on the metal housing of the back, emphasizing the product’s ease of use and reliability. Contemporary Graflex catalogues promoted the RITEWAY back as a “ready‑way” solution for field and studio work, highlighting its quick‑change capability. The registration on 1934-01-04 formalized Graflex’s claim to the name in connection with these accessories.

## The Connection

The patented camera back provides the mechanical basis for the RITEWAY product line, as the trademark specifically covers film and plate holders that embody the patented latch and sealing system. By securing the patented features within a branded accessory, Graflex could differentiate its offering from competing backs that lacked the same precision and speed of operation. The close temporal proximity—patent grant in March 1933 and trademark filing in January 1934—suggests a deliberate strategy to commercialize the invention under the RITEWAY name. Thus, the patent enables the functional performance that the trademarked product promises.

## Historical Context

During the early 1930s, large‑format photography was transitioning from studio‑bound practice to more mobile field work, creating demand for faster film handling solutions. Graflex, already a leading maker of press‑type cameras, responded by engineering detachable backs that reduced exposure downtime, a competitive advantage in news and documentary photography. The Great Depression era also pressured manufacturers to offer value‑added accessories that could extend the utility of existing camera bodies, making the RITEWAY back both a technical and economic innovation. The filing aligns with a broader industry trend toward modular camera systems in the pre‑World War II period.

This pair exemplifies how Graflex leveraged patent protection to underpin a branded accessory that addressed a specific workflow need in professional photography. It illustrates the interplay between technical invention (the detachable back) and commercial branding (the RITEWAY mark) within a narrow time window, highlighting the company’s strategic use of intellectual property.
