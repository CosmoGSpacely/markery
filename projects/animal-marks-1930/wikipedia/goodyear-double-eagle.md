## Supporting notes

**Why this pair:** The DOUBLE EAGLE mark is the strongest animal-imagery case in the project with a confirmed patent connection. Goodyear filed the trademark within one year of the patent grant, consistent with the project's central argument that patent and trademark filings were coordinated instruments of commercial strategy. Both primary sources are publicly verifiable via TSDR and Google Patents.

**Public domain status:** Serial 71273140 has status code 900 (dead/abandoned). The mark drawing is public domain under US copyright law — works published in 1928 are unconditionally in the public domain as of January 1, 2026. The eagle imagery in the DOUBLE EAGLE mark may be uploadable to Wikimedia Commons for use in the article.

**Patent:** US1645089A (Solid Vehicle Tire, granted 1927-10-11, assignee GOODYEAR TIRE & RUBBER). Available at https://patents.google.com/patent/US1645089A

**Trademark:** Serial 71273140, filed 1928-09-29, registered as No. 0251857. Goods: PNEUMATIC TIRES CONSTRUCTED WHOLLY OR PARTLY OF RUBBER AND USED FOR MOTOR TRUCKS, MOTOR CARS, MOTORCYCLES, BICYCLES, AEROPLANES, AND OTHER VEHICLES.

**Goods description alignment:** The trademark's cross-vehicle goods description (trucks, cars, motorcycles, bicycles, aeroplanes) matches the 1927 patent's broad vehicle scope, confirming that DOUBLE EAGLE was positioned as a product-line brand for the full commercial tire range enabled by the patented construction method.

**Historical framing:** Presbrey (1929, *The History and Development of Advertising*) documents that animal names and emblems in American brand marks signaled strength and reliability — the eagle specifically conveying national pride and industrial power. Goodyear's choice of a double eagle emblem for a tire line positioned for the expanding American automotive and aviation markets of the late 1920s fits this pattern.
