## The Invention

Patent US1829460A, titled "Hot Cathode Electron Discharge Tube," was applied for on August 14, 1926, and granted on October 27, 1931, to Westinghouse Electric & Mfg Co. The invention operates within CPC class H01J—electric discharge tubes and discharge lamps. A hot cathode electron discharge tube employs a heated filament to emit electrons through thermionic emission; these electrons are then directed and controlled by electrode geometry and applied electric potentials. The underlying physics of this mechanism appears in both incandescent lamp filaments and the active vacuum tubes employed in radio receivers, where the distinction between a "lamp" and a "tube" lies primarily in application rather than fundamental physical principle. The five-year prosecution interval from 1926 to 1931 reflects both the technical complexity of the patent claims and the crowded patent landscape in the H01J class during the radio manufacturing boom.

## The Mark

MINALITE (Trademark Serial No. 71321058) was filed by Westinghouse Electric and Manufacturing Company on November 11, 1931, fifteen days after US1829460A reached patent grant. The trademark specification identifies "incandescent electric lamps" as the covered goods, and the mark was registered as No. 0294205. MINALITE was Westinghouse's commercial designation for its line of miniature incandescent lamps—compact, low-wattage filament sources designed for instrument panels, appliance indicator lights, and emerging consumer electronics applications. The rapid succession of patent grant and trademark filing suggests coordinated market introduction strategy across Westinghouse's product line.

## Historical Context

The early 1930s marked a critical phase in miniature lamp technology development. By the mid-1920s, radio receiver penetration in American homes had created substantial demand for compact indicator lamps suitable for tuning dials and signal-strength meters. Westinghouse, General Electric, and RCA all competed actively in this emerging market segment. MINALITE represented Westinghouse's branded product entry into miniature lamps for instruments and consumer appliances. The H01J patent classification encompasses both discharge tubes and incandescent lamps, reflecting the underlying engineering congruence: a miniature incandescent lamp and a radio vacuum tube occupy adjacent points on the spectrum of heated-cathode vacuum devices. Westinghouse's patent prosecution activity throughout the 1920s and 1930s within this class addressed the full range of hot-cathode applications available to manufacturers.
