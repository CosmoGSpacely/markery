## The Invention

Patent US1829460A, titled "Hot Cathode Electron Discharge Tube" and assigned to Westinghouse Electric & Mfg Co, was applied for on August 14, 1926, and granted October 27, 1931. It falls in CPC class H01J — electric discharge tubes and discharge lamps. A hot cathode electron discharge tube uses a heated filament to emit electrons thermionically; the emitted electrons are then directed and controlled by electrode geometry or applied potentials. This is the physical mechanism underlying both incandescent lamp filaments and the active vacuum tubes used in radio receivers — a "lamp" and a "tube" differ in application but share the same electron-emission physics. The five-year prosecution period (1926–1931) reflects the complexity of the claims and the competitive patent landscape in the H01J class during the height of the radio manufacturing era.

## The Mark

MINALITE (Trademark Serial No. 71321058) was filed by Westinghouse Electric Corporation on November 11, 1931 — fifteen days after US1829460A was granted. The goods statement covers "incandescent electric lamps." MINALITE was Westinghouse's trade name for its miniature incandescent lamp line — compact, low-wattage filament lamps used in instrument panels, appliance indicator lights, and consumer electronics. The trademark was registered as No. 0294205.

## The Connection

US1829460A and the MINALITE trademark are linked through Westinghouse's hot cathode engineering work. The lamp engineering group that developed MINALITE miniature lamps and the vacuum tube engineering group that produced H01J discharge tube patents operated from the same technical base: thermionic emission from a heated filament. The fifteen-day filing gap between patent grant and trademark filing indicates coordinated commercial launch alongside IP registration — the same coordination pattern visible in the later STERILAMP pair. The patent filing date of August 1926 corresponds to the peak years of American radio manufacturing, when Westinghouse's electrical engineering divisions were at maximum research output.

## Historical Context

The early 1930s were a formative period for miniature lamp technology. Radio receivers had proliferated in American homes by the mid-1920s, and manufacturers needed compact indicator lamps for tuning dials and signal strength meters. Westinghouse, GE, and RCA all competed in this market. MINALITE was Westinghouse's branded entry into miniature lamps for instruments and consumer appliances. The H01J patent class covers both discharge tubes and incandescent lamps, reflecting the engineering commonality: a miniature incandescent lamp and a radio vacuum tube are structurally similar vacuum devices with heated filament cathodes. Westinghouse's patent prosecution in this class across the 1920s and 1930s covered the full spectrum of hot-cathode applications.

## Significance

This pair demonstrates that Westinghouse's patent-trademark coordination extended throughout its electrical product portfolio. The 15-day gap between patent grant and trademark filing is among the tightest in the radio-pioneers dataset, indicating planned commercial launch. The MINALITE-US1829460A pair also illustrates a finding of the project: the manufacturers most active in the radio patent pool (RCA, Westinghouse, GE) applied the same patent-to-market strategy across their entire electrical portfolios, not only in the radio category specifically.
