## The Invention

Patent US2168861A, titled "Stroboscopic Lamp" and assigned to Westinghouse Electric & Mfg Co, was applied for in 1934 and granted on August 8, 1939. The patent falls in CPC class H01J — electric discharge tubes and discharge lamps — covering gas-filled discharge tube designs. A stroboscopic lamp produces brief, intense flashes of light by discharging electrical energy through a gas (typically mercury vapor or rare gas) in rapid pulses. The technology required precise control of gas pressure, electrode geometry, and discharge characteristics to achieve the required pulse duration and spectral output.

## The Mark

STERILAMP (Trademark Serial No. 71423019) was filed by Westinghouse Electric Corporation on August 25, 1939 — seventeen days after the stroboscopic lamp patent was granted. The goods statement covers "vacuum or gas-filled tubes employed for the generation of radiations for hastening the tenderization of meat, in showcases and domestic refrigerators." The STERILAMP product was Westinghouse's branded ultraviolet germicidal lamp, sold to food retailers and cold storage operators to inhibit bacterial growth. Both ultraviolet germicidal lamps and stroboscopic lamps belong to the same H01J discharge tube class: they are gas-filled or evacuated tubes in which controlled electrical discharge produces radiant energy. The trademark was registered as No. 0375857.

## The Connection

US2168861A and the STERILAMP trademark share the same underlying discharge tube technology. Westinghouse's research into gas discharge lamp design in the 1930s — covering both pulsed stroboscopic applications and continuous ultraviolet output — produced patents and products from the same engineering team and manufacturing line. The seventeen-day gap between patent grant and trademark filing reflects Westinghouse's practice of coordinating patent prosecution with commercial trademark registration as a product reached market readiness. The stroboscopic patent, filed in 1934, represents five years of refinement that culminated in both the stroboscopic lamp product and the parallel STERILAMP UV product commercialized in 1939.

## Historical Context

By 1939 Westinghouse was a major force in specialty lamp technology, competing with General Electric in both incandescent and discharge lamp categories. The late 1930s saw rapid commercial expansion of ultraviolet germicidal lamps following public health interest in food safety and the growing cold-storage industry. Westinghouse positioned STERILAMP specifically for meat display cases and refrigerators — a targeted industrial market where UV radiation could extend shelf life without chemical preservatives. The patent-trademark filing sequence in August 1939 marks the commercial launch of this product line.

## Significance

This pair illustrates how Westinghouse used the patent-trademark coordination strategy to protect both the technical invention (H01J discharge tube design) and the commercial brand (STERILAMP) simultaneously. The 17-day filing gap is among the shortest in the radio-pioneers candidate pool, indicating deliberate coordination between Westinghouse's patent and trademark counsel. It also demonstrates the breadth of Westinghouse's electrical product portfolio: even within the radio-era vacuum tube technology base, the company was developing consumer and industrial applications beyond radio reception, from germicidal lamps to stroboscopic timing devices.
