Yawman & Erbe

Historical Identity

Yawman & Erbe Mfg. Co. of Rochester, New York was one of the earliest American manufacturers of vertical filing equipment. Founded in the 1890s, the company developed filing cabinets, file guides, and associated equipment in the period when the vertical filing system was displacing older flat-file and pigeon-hole office furniture. By the early twentieth century, Yawman & Erbe was a recognized name in the American office-supply trade, with a patent record extending from 1902 through the research period.

The company registered three trademarks in the 1900–1939 period covered by this project: SHANNON (January 23, 1930) and two "Y AND E." marks (both filed in February 1930). All three were filed within a six-week window, indicating a coordinated portfolio registration at the start of the decade. The SHANNON mark is the only confirmed pair in the project involving an acquired brand name rather than a product invented by the registrant.

Yawman & Erbe was later absorbed into larger consolidations of the office-equipment industry, as the filing-cabinet market matured and manufacturer count contracted.

In the Filing Record

Yawman & Erbe holds 59 patents in filing-appliance classes across the research period, with filings beginning in 1902 and continuing through the 1930s. The patent portfolio covers file guides, file folders, cabinet mechanisms, and related vertical-file components — the mechanical infrastructure of the open-shelf and drawer-based filing systems the company sold. This is a large patent record for a manufacturer that does not appear in the project's first tier of entities, reflecting the company's early entry into the field and its sustained technical activity across three decades.

The three trademarks all date to January–February 1930. SHANNON (serial 71295144, registration 0272881) covered arch-type binders and related arch-file equipment. The two "Y AND E." marks (serials 71297112 and 71297345) covered loose-leaf binders and filing-cabinet equipment respectively — the company's own branded line rather than the SHANNON acquisition.

On the patent side, US1738120A ("File Folder," December 3, 1929) precedes the SHANNON trademark filing by 51 days, providing the confirmed pair for this entity.

Research Significance

Yawman & Erbe is the project's example of a filing-equipment manufacturer with a long patent history and a distinct brand strategy: registering the SHANNON name, which originated with Shannon Brothers Ltd. of London in the 1880s, as an American trademark in 1930. The SHANNON pair is the only confirmed pair in the project where the trademark registers an acquired name rather than a name the trademark owner invented. The correspondence between US1738120A and the SHANNON mark is by product category — both concern filing equipment — rather than by direct claim coverage, because the patent describes a file folder while the trademark covers arch-type binders.

The January–February 1930 cluster of three trademark filings, combined with the December 1929 patent grant, documents a six-week period of intellectual property activity at Yawman & Erbe: one patent issued, three trademarks filed. This compressed timeline suggests deliberate portfolio management at the turn of the decade, consistent with the behavior seen at Wilson Jones in September 1927 and at Remington Rand in 1939.
