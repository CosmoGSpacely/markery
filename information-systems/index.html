About This Project

The information-systems project cross-references the USPTO patent record with the USPTO trademark filing record for four companies that dominated the American office-filing market between 1900 and 1939: Remington Rand, Wilson Jones, Yawman & Erbe, and Boorum & Pease. The central question is whether a company's patent grants and trademark filings for the same period document a coordinated intellectual property strategy — and if so, how close the temporal correspondence is between the two filing streams.

The research method is straightforward. A patent grant date and a trademark filing date, for the same entity, covering goods that correspond to the patent's claims, constitute a candidate pair. A confirmed pair requires three elements in alignment: the same assignee entity on both records, a temporal gap consistent with commercial coordination (days to months, not decades), and a goods-to-claims match sufficient to place both records in the same product line. Eight confirmed pairs meet these criteria across the four entities.

What the Record Shows

Remington Rand is the project's central case: 171 patents and a trademark portfolio that documents the full arc of visible-card-index technology from 1918 through 1939. The company's confirmed pairs include the Russell and Odell Soundex patents (1918, 1922) linked to the SOUNDEX and SOUNDEX QUICK AS A FLASH trademarks (both 1927), and US2152606A and US2178457A linked to the VARIADEX and KARDEX marks filed in 1939.

Wilson Jones provides the project's clearest examples of coordinated product-family launches. The September 1927 cluster — VI-DEX filed in July, REDIREF and HANDIREF filed together in September — registered three product-name trademarks in a ten-week window, each anchored by a George W. Newman binder patent from the preceding two years.

Yawman & Erbe offers a distinct pattern: the SHANNON mark registers an acquired brand name (Shannon Brothers Ltd., London, 1880s) rather than a product invented by the registrant. The correspondence between US1738120A and the SHANNON trademark is by product category rather than by direct claim coverage.

Boorum & Pease holds patents and registered trademarks across the same period but shows no confirmed patent-trademark pair. The company's trademark filings are house marks and quality designations, not mechanism-specific product names — a different brand strategy that does not generate the patent-trademark correspondence documented in the other three entities.
